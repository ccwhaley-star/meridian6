How to Stop Wasting Money on Google Ads

← Insights  ·  Meridian6  ·  3 min read

Google makes it very easy to give Google money. Sign up, type your business name, set a budget, and Smart Mode handles the rest. What it handles, mostly, is spending your budget — on clicks you can't see, for searches you'd never have chosen, measured by metrics that flatter the platform.

Smart Mode is a donation

Smart campaigns hide the levers that make ads profitable: you can't see full search terms, can't exclude junk traffic properly, and can't control where ads show. It's designed for people who won't manage their ads — and priced accordingly. If you've run Smart campaigns and concluded “Google Ads doesn't work for my business,” you haven't actually run Google Ads yet.

The four levers that make ads pay

1. Expert Mode. Same platform, all the controls. This is the only mode we run for clients — and the only mode we've ever run for our own businesses.

2. Negative keywords. The fastest savings in advertising. If you're a lawn care company, you do not want clicks from “lawn care jobs,” “free lawn care,” or “lawn care school.” Every irrelevant search you exclude is budget redirected to buyers.

3. Conversion tracking. If you don't track calls and form fills, you're optimizing for clicks — and clicks are not customers. Tracking turns “we spent $800” into “we spent $800 and got 19 calls,” which is a decision you can actually make.

4. Tight match types. Broad match spends adventurously. Phrase and exact match spend on people searching for what you actually sell.

The honest math

A well-run local campaign should tell you, in plain numbers, what a lead costs you. When you know a customer costs $40 in ads and is worth $400 in revenue, scaling is arithmetic, not faith. That's the entire point — ads you can read like a P&L.
