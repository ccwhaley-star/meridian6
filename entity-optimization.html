Entity Optimization: How Google Decides Who Your Business Is

← Insights  ·  Meridian6  ·  4 min read

Before Google ranks your business, it has to understand it. Who are you? What do you do? Where do you do it? Is the information about you the same everywhere it appears? Google treats your business as an entity — a known thing in the real world — and the more confident it is about that entity, the more willing it is to show you to searchers.

Entity optimization is the work of removing every doubt. It isn't glamorous, and it doesn't happen in one afternoon. But it's the foundation every other local SEO tactic sits on, and it's the first thing we check on every engagement.

Why this matters more than it used to

Google's map pack, its AI Overviews, and assistants like ChatGPT all rely on the same underlying question: how sure are we that this business is real, relevant, and located where it says? A business with clean, consistent signals gets recommended. A business with three different phone numbers floating around the web, an old address on Yelp, and no structured data gets skipped — even if its work is better.

The signals that build an entity

Google Business Profile. The primary signal. Your name, address, phone, categories, and hours here are the canonical version of who you are. Set it up first, and treat it as the source of truth.

Your website. Clear service pages, a real location, and your name, address, and phone (NAP) in the footer, written exactly as they appear on your profile.

Structured data. JSON-LD schema tells Google in machine language what your business is: a LocalBusiness, and more specifically a florist, a plumber, a deli. It's invisible to visitors and one of the most direct entity signals you can send.

Consistent NAP. Name, address, and phone identical everywhere. “Suite 200” on one site and “#200” on another is minor; two different phone numbers is not.

Citations and directories. Yelp, Bing Places, Apple Maps, and the major data aggregators. Each one that matches reinforces the entity. Each one that conflicts weakens it.

Social profiles. Real, active profiles linked back to your site (and to each other in your schema) confirm that the brand exists beyond its own website.

Reviews. Steady, genuine reviews are customers validating the entity in public. A simple ask after every job beats any review campaign.

PR, industry listings, and backlinks. A mention in local news, a listing with your trade association, a link from a partner's site. Each is a trusted third party vouching that you are who you say you are.

The fastest fix: a consistency audit

Search your business name and phone number in Google. Look at every listing on the first two pages. Write down every version of your name, address, and phone you find. If they don't all match your Google Business Profile exactly, that's your to-do list. Old addresses from a previous location and tracking numbers from an old marketing vendor are the most common culprits.

What it pays back

Done right, entity optimization shows up as higher map rankings, more visibility in AI answers, more credibility with first-time visitors, and ultimately more calls. It's slow, foundational work — which is exactly why most of your competitors haven't done it.
